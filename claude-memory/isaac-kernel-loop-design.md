**Owner, 2026-07-13:** "isaac is a main simple loop saying check the
net. but the route through the net should shift and adapt and change
over time so isaac today may not look like the isaac we started with."
All functions he uses should be accessible within his net — able to
**access, modify, or bypass anything through the structure itself**.

**Standing grievance (do not repeat the mistake):** at some point the
files got split up, "which i specifically said not to do" — the split
put capabilities outside the structure where Isaac cannot reach them.
Owner: "something we will need to readdress at some point." NOT
urgent-now, but never split further, and the readdress is on the books.

The kernel design:
- kernel = minimal loop; everything else = net content
- methods/organs = callable nodes with doors + use-receipts
  ([[self-learning-is-the-goal]] — improved methods are learnable)
- the execution route IS net content → it rewrites itself over time;
  Isaac drifting away from launch-Isaac is the INTENDED outcome
- the interpreter package's match→select→act→write-back cycle is this
  exact loop; its rules are the exact node-shape for organs

**How to apply:** when building, the structure-of-record is the NET,
not the filesystem — every capability must have a node/door in the net
regardless of where the Python lives (wrap-as-nodes may restore what
the file split broke without a re-merge; owner's call at readdress
time). Never add a capability only as an external script he can't
reach. [[wisdom-laws]] [[design-intent-sheet]]
